LEXFIT · Dashboard skeleton · Profil & beállítások

Where the user goes to feel in control.

Profile is the least-visited and most-trusted screen in a product: people arrive here when something needs changing, and they arrive slightly anxious. So it is the one place where convention matters more than character — the settings grammar of iOS, Facebook and Google, in plain Hungarian, with nothing hidden and nothing clever.

P1

The entry point — avatar, top-right, opens a menu

The audit found LEXFIT's profile at the foot of the left sidebar. The circular avatar in the top-right corner is one of the most consistently reinforced positions on the web — Google, Facebook, Netflix, Spotify — and Jakob's Law applies most strongly to position, not styling. Clicking it opens a menu, not a page: the user stays where they were.

F-04

Avatar moves from bottom-left to top-right. The most engraved position on the web; the audit flagged the sidebar placement as a P2 identity issue.

A menu, not a navigation

Profile is not a fifth destination. Opening a menu over the current page means the user never loses their place — and the four tabs stay four.

Six items, plain words

Icon plus a permanently visible Hungarian label on every row. NN/g: labels are particularly critical wherever an icon has to carry meaning.

Kijelentkezés last, and coloured

Destructive or session-ending actions sit at the bottom, visually separated. Universal convention — and it prevents mis-taps.

P2

Profil — who I am and how I'm doing

Not a settings page. This is the mirror: identity, the streak, the programme, the numbers. Everything editable lives one click away in Beállítások, so this screen can stay calm and readable — the layered-complexity principle from fitness-platform practice: beginner-friendly surface, depth on demand.

Numbers, not charts

Three big tabular figures. The detailed view lives in Haladásom — duplicating it here would create two answers to the same question.

"Miért kezdted"

The user's own sentence from onboarding, shown back to them. No reference app has this; it is the one place on the page where LEXFIT gets to be itself.

Streak beside the name

Identity and habit in the same row — the Duolingo model, where the streak is part of who you are rather than a statistic.

No vanity metrics

No calories, no badge wall, no ranking. Nothing that invites comparison with other people on a page about oneself.

P3

Beállítások — grouped rows, nothing hidden

The settings grammar everyone already knows: a labelled group, rows of icon + label, a value or a toggle on the right, a chevron when the row leads somewhere. Desktop gets a secondary sub-nav on the left — the Google / Facebook account pattern — so the page never becomes a single endless scroll.

The iOS Settings grammar

Group label in small caps, rows with icon + label, current value on the right, chevron when it opens something. Every phone in Hungary ships with this pattern.

Toggles on the right, always

Left-aligned label, right-aligned control. Mixed alignment is where settings pages start feeling unfamiliar.

Say what a setting does

"Ez határozza meg, mi kerül a kezdőlapra". NN/g heuristic 2 — speak the user's language and never assume a label carries its consequence.

Rest day toggle

The forgiveness mechanic made explicit and user-controlled. Freeze-equipped streaks reportedly run ~48% longer past day seven.

Deletion is findable

Account deletion is present, plainly worded, with its consequence stated. Burying it is a documented dark pattern — and now illegal in some jurisdictions.

P4

Emlékeztetők, Adatvédelem, Előfizetés

Three panels that carry disproportionate weight: the habit lever, the trust contract, and the money. Each is drawn in the same grouped-row grammar so nothing feels like a different product.

One daily reminder, on your days

Duolingo's playbook without the pressure: a single trigger tied to the plan the user already set. Not a stream of notifications.

"Sorozat veszélyben"

The one extra notification that earns its place — it protects something the user has already invested in, rather than advertising something new.

Privacy states the consequence

Each toggle says what happens when it is off. Fitness apps carry real privacy anxiety; vague permission language is what breaks trust.

Progress photos are not a toggle

They are stated as private, permanently. A setting implies it could be otherwise — this one cannot.

Cancellation in plain sight

Renewal date, price and a cancel row that says what happens after. The existing sales-page audit already flagged price clarity as a P1.

P5

Mobile — sheet, profile, settings, reminder

Four tabs is the sweet spot, so profile stays off the tab bar. The avatar in the top bar opens a sheet in the thumb zone; from there it is full screens with a back chevron, exactly like the phone's own Settings app.

Sheet first, screens after

The menu opens in the thumb zone; anything it leads to is a full screen with a back chevron top-left — the phone's own Settings behaviour.

Rows are 48px on mobile

Above the 44px floor even with two lines of text. Settings rows are where mis-taps are most expensive.

Time presets before a picker

Four suggested times cover most users in one tap; the full picker stays available. Typing and scroll wheels are expensive on a phone.

Tab bar stays visible

Even inside settings. The user can always leave without hunting for a way out — and the four destinations remain the map of the product.

P6

Profil rules

P-RULE 01

Avatar top-right, opening a menu.

Same position on desktop and mobile. Never a fifth tab, never a sidebar row.

EvidenceAudit finding F-04; the most consistently reinforced position across Google, Facebook, Netflix, Spotify.
P-RULE 02

Profil shows, Beállítások edits.

One page to look at yourself, one to change things. Never mix the two.

EvidenceLayered complexity — beginner-friendly surface, depth on demand.
P-RULE 03

Grouped rows: label left, value or toggle right.

Small-caps group heading, icon + label, chevron only when the row opens something.

EvidenceThe iOS Settings grammar — the pattern every phone in Hungary ships with.
P-RULE 04

Every setting states its consequence.

Not "Közösségi láthatóság" but "Kikapcsolva „R." néven jelensz meg".

EvidenceNN/g heuristic 2 — speak the users' language; never assume a label carries its effect.
P-RULE 05

Money and deletion are findable and plain.

Renewal date, price, cancel row, delete account — all present, all worded honestly.

EvidenceDark-pattern avoidance; price clarity was already a P1 on the existing sales-page audit.
P-RULE 06

One daily notification, tied to the user's own plan.

Plus one streak-protection message. Nothing else is on by default.

EvidenceDuolingo's habit loop paired with forgiveness; permission language must state why.

What we are deliberately not doing

✕A badge wall or trophy cabinet. Achievements that mean nothing outside the app dilute the one number that does: the streak.
✕Public leaderboards on the profile. A page about oneself should not invite comparison; solo experience first, social as an optional layer.
✕Weight as a headline number. Progress is measured against week 1, not against a scale — consistent with the programme's own framing.
✕Hiding cancellation behind support chat. A documented dark pattern, and a trust-destroying one for a subscription product.
✕Notifications on by default beyond the daily reminder. Every extra default is borrowed attention the product has not earned.
✕A separate "account" area outside the app shell. Settings live inside the same rail and tab bar as everything else.
Still to settle with real users

Two questions: whether the profile should show the "Miért kezdted" sentence permanently or only around milestones — it may land as motivating or as nagging — and whether "Előfizetés" belongs directly in the avatar menu, since for many users it is the only reason they ever open this area.
