LEXFIT · Dashboard skeleton · Kihívások — a Szavazz Magadra archívum

An archive, not a feed.

Correction to the earlier draft. The voting and the posting happen in the Facebook group — that is where the community already lives, and the product should not try to move it. What belongs here is the archive: every weekly Szavazz Magadra challenge the group has already been through, browsable, sortable, and completable alone at any time. That makes this a second library, not a social network — so it inherits the Videótár logic, not the Facebook one.

Unit · a challenge = a multi-day series Model · playlist / album — Spotify, YouTube Browse · rows → grid, the Videótár switch Social · stays on Facebook
C1

What changed, and what it implies

A feed and an archive are different products with different conventions. Getting this right removes a whole category of work — no composer, no moderation, no reactions — and replaces it with a pattern the audience already knows even better than the Facebook one.

A korábbi feltételezés
Közösségi hírfolyam a termékben

Posts, reactions, a composer, moderation, privacy defaults. Facebook's grammar rebuilt inside LEXFIT — duplicating a place the members are already in, and asking them to be active in two.

A valóság
A kihívások archívuma

Every past weekly challenge, kept, sorted and re-playable. The Facebook group stays the live room; the app becomes the shelf you take things off — alone, whenever.

Következmény 01
Ez egy második könyvtár.

So it inherits Videótár's rules wholesale: rows while browsing, grid on intent, visible filter chips, count before grid.

Következmény 02
A tétel egy sorozat, nem egy videó.

A challenge is 5–14 days. That is a playlist — Spotify album, YouTube playlist, Netflix series. The card and the detail page follow that grammar.

Következmény 03
A közösség egy link.

One honest way out to the Facebook group where the voting happens. No feed, no composer, no reactions inside the product.

Label follows content

With the feed gone, "Közösség" is the wrong word for this destination. Drawn here as Kihívások — the plain word for what is inside. "Szavazz Magadra" stays as the name of the series.

Cheaper and more familiar

The archive pattern is better known to this audience than a social feed and costs less to build. Both arguments point the same way.

Icon changes too

People (users) no longer describes the destination. A trophy or flag glyph is the conventional archive-of-challenges mark.

One thing is lost

The in-product sense of "I'm not alone". Where does that now live? Options: participant counts on a challenge, the Facebook link-out, or Alexa's line on the detail page. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by omission.

C2

The unit — a challenge card is a series card

A workout card promises one session; a challenge card promises a commitment. The difference has to be legible at thumbnail size, and every app the audience uses solves it the same way: a stack mark, a count of parts, and a progress bar across the whole series.

Stack mark, top-left

"7 RÉSZ" with a layers glyph — the universal signal that an item contains other items. Playlists, albums and series all mark it in the same corner.

Duration is in days, not minutes

The badge keeps its engraved bottom-right position but changes unit. For a challenge the commitment is calendar length; per-day minutes belong on the detail page.

Progress spans the series

"5 / 14 nap kész" with the bar across the whole challenge. Same Netflix borrowing as the workout card, one level up.

Title only, state below

Consistent with Variant B: heading plus a line that appears only when there is state to report — here the month, the progress, or the completion.

C3

The archive — browse and sort

Identical two-mode logic to Videótár: rows while browsing, a grid the moment a filter or sort is applied. The filter dimensions are different because the questions are different — how long a commitment, what it works, and whether I have done it already.

V-RULE 01, reused

Intent switches the mode. Any filter or sort → grid; clear everything → rows. The user learns this once and it works in two destinations.

"Állapot" is the new dimension

Elkezdetlen / folyamatban / kész. In an archive the most useful filter is "what haven't I done yet" — Videótár has no equivalent because a workout is repeatable by nature.

Sort defaults to newest

An archive is read backwards from now. "Legrövidebb elől" is the second most useful, for the user browsing by commitment size.

The Facebook link is honest and last

One row at the foot of the page explaining the division of labour. Not a banner, not a modal — the app is not competing with the group.

Row budget again

Three rows plus the link-out. With 38 challenges the temptation is a row per theme; that would make the page longer than the archive is deep.

C4

A challenge — the playlist page

Cover, title, what it is, one loud button, then a numbered list of days. This is the Spotify album page and the YouTube playlist page, and it is the correct grammar for anything with an intended order. Days already done are checked; the next one is highlighted; later days stay open — a challenge is not a locked corridor.

The album page, unchanged

Cover left, title and metadata right, one filled play button, numbered list below. Spotify and YouTube both do exactly this, and so does every podcast app on the phone.

"Folytatás — 6. nap"

The button names the next action rather than the object. A returning user never has to work out where they stopped.

Later days are not locked

Ordered, not gated. Locking days would punish the user who has ten minutes today and twenty tomorrow — and this archive exists precisely for self-paced use.

Completed days stay playable

Greyed label, check in the index column, play control still live. Done is not spent.

Where "not alone" now lives

"312-en csinálták végig a csoporttal" — a fact about the past, not a leaderboard. This is the one place the social dimension survives inside the product.

Archive rows with a peek, then the playlist page as a full screen. Because a challenge runs alongside the Foundation plan rather than replacing it, the Kezdőlap keeps its own hero — the challenge appears there only as a "Folytatod" row entry.

Cover, title, chips, one full-width button in the thumb zone, then the day list. Identical to how the phone's own podcast and music apps present a series.

C6

Kihívások rules

C-RULE 01

This is an archive, and it behaves like Videótár.

Rows while browsing, grid on intent, visible filter chips, count before grid.

EvidenceV-RULE 01–04, already established. Two destinations, one learned behaviour.
C-RULE 02

A challenge is a playlist.

Stack mark and part count on the card; cover, metadata, one play button and a numbered list on the page.

EvidenceSpotify album / YouTube playlist — the universal grammar for ordered collections.
C-RULE 03

Ordered, never gated.

Days are numbered and the next one is highlighted, but every day is playable at any time.

EvidenceThe archive exists for self-paced solo use; locking contradicts its reason to exist.
C-RULE 04

Duration in days on the card, minutes on the page.

The commitment question comes first; the per-session question comes second.

EvidenceBadge keeps its engraved position; only the unit changes with the object.
C-RULE 05

The Facebook group is linked, not imitated.

One honest row explaining where voting happens, plus a participant count per challenge.

EvidenceThe community already exists elsewhere; duplicating it splits it and moderates nothing.
C-RULE 06

A challenge runs alongside Foundation, never replaces it.

The home hero stays the programme; the challenge appears in Folytatod.

EvidenceOne primary commitment per user; two competing "today" answers on the home screen is the failure mode.

What we are deliberately not doing

✕An in-product feed, composer or reactions. The room is on Facebook. Building a second one splits the conversation and creates a moderation surface nobody owns.
✕Locking day 4 until day 3 is done. Ordered guidance, not a corridor.
✕Leaderboards of who finished a challenge fastest. A participant count is a fact; a ranking is a competition.
✕Expiring challenges. The whole point of an archive is that nothing expires — a past week is a permanent asset.
✕Letting a challenge hijack the Kezdőlap hero. Foundation is the product's spine; challenges are parallel.
✕Importing Facebook comments into the app. Read-only mirrors of a live conversation are stale by definition and imply a reply box that does not exist.
Still to settle

Three: whether the destination is called Kihívások (drawn here) or keeps the Szavazz Magadra name; whether a challenge should be able to reschedule the Foundation week or simply sit beside it; and where the "I'm not alone" feeling now lives, since the feed is gone — the participant count is a start, but it may not be enough on its own.
