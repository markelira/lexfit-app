## A1 · Section-by-section notes

**1 · Hero — `HOOK`**
Promise, the price line, a trust row (10 év versenysport · 1 200+ tag · 14 napos
elállási jog), one CTA. Strong conversion because it states the offer and the price
above the fold. Trust only medium: the trust row is assertions, unproven at this point.

**2 · Hogyan működik — `MECHANISM`** ★ *strongest all-round section on the page*
Three real app screenshots: the adaptation question → the finished week → the player.
It is the only band that scores H on all three. It converts (it makes the CTA legible
— you know what "Összeállítom a tervem" *does*), it builds trust (real screens, not
mockups), and it helps decide (you can see the actual product). Its kicker — *"A heted
azelőtt látod, hogy fiókot csinálnál"* — is a risk-remover doing work no other line does.

**3 · Amikor kész vagy — `PAYOFF` + `SOCIAL PROOF`**
Finish Share. Real consented member photos, illustrative numbers. High trust: seven
faces is the most human evidence on the page. Low decision-help: nobody chooses a
fitness app because of a share feature. Conversion medium — it sells a feeling, not a
reason.

**4 · Edzés Alexával — `PRODUCT REALITY` + `OBJECTION`**
What a session actually is, plus the adaptation chips (Csendes · Falra fogva · …) and
the looping player recording. H on all three counts *and* it defuses the single
biggest silent objection for home training in a flat — noise and neighbours. The
Csendes category is the one thing no Hungarian competitor communicates.

**5 · Nagy képernyő — `FEATURE`** ⚠ *weakest section on the page*
Cast to TV / laptop. Nobody subscribes because of Chromecast. It removes a small
friction for people already convinced. It is also 1,062px — the second-largest
band-to-value ratio on the page.

**6 · Programok — `SCOPE`**
7 programs, 47 workouts, real app components. High conversion and decision value: it
is the "how much am I actually getting" answer. Trust only medium — counts are
verifiable but abstract.

**7 · Ár-horgony — `PRICE`**
490 Ft first week. Placed here deliberately so the number lands while the catalog is
still on screen. Shortest band on the page (539px) and earns its place.

**8 · Foundation — `ENTRY POINT`**
"Innen indulsz." The animated journey through the entry program. Its real job is
risk-removal: *you will not have to work out where to begin.* Highest decision value,
moderate conversion.

**9 · A heted — `DIFFERENTIATOR`**
The interactive week picker. This is the structural advantage over every fixed PDF
plan, and the only section a visitor can *touch*. Answers the most common objection
("nincs rá időm") with a mechanism rather than a reassurance.

**10 · Kihívások — `COMMUNITY`**
The Facebook group card + 15-challenge archive. High trust (1 200+ real members, an
existing thing that predates the app), low decision value.

**11 · Alexa — `AUTHORITY`**
The founder story. The page's emotional peak and its single largest band (1,394px).
Highest trust contribution of any section. Low direct decision value — it does not
tell you whether the product fits your Tuesday.

**12 · GYIK — `OBJECTION HANDLING`**
Ten questions including the two that matter most commercially: *"Miért fizessek, ha a
YouTube-on ingyen is van?"* and *"Hogyan mondhatom le?"*. High trust and decision value,
and the cancellation answer is doing quiet conversion work.

**13 · Előfizetés — `CLOSE`**
Three plans, terms stated, no fake strikethroughs.

# PART B — Research

## B1 · The golden thread

The golden thread is **one idea, carried unbroken from the first line to the last**.
It is not a structure — it is the single message every section is a different proof of.

> *"Copywriters know you should build your entire promotion around ONE idea. ONE
> message."* — [FocusCopy](https://focuscopy.com/how-to-write-a-golden-thread/)

Two properties matter for ordering:

**It is emotional, not logical.** AWAI's framing is that the thread is *"the tug on your
prospect's emotions… your goal is to keep your prospect's emotions high from beginning
to end"* ([AWAI](https://www.awai.com/2006/05/the-golden-thread/)). A section that
drops emotional temperature breaks the thread even if it is factually well-placed.

**It requires the promise to be restated, not just stated once.** *"You must pepper your
copy with your promise, the benefits, and your USP throughout"*
([Salish Sea](https://www.salishseaconsulting.com/blog/golden-thread-copywriting/)).
So repeating the core idea across sections is the mechanism, not redundancy.

## B2 · The slippery slide — the sentence-to-sentence handoff

This is the mechanic behind your phrase *"like words do in sentences."* Joe Sugarman:

> *"The only purpose of each sentence is to get the reader to read the next sentence."*
> — [Upgrow](https://www.upgrow.io/joe-sugarman-slippery-slide-landing-page-style/)

Two techniques scale up from sentences to sections:

- **Seeds of curiosity** — end a unit with a tease that makes the next one necessary.
- **"Copywriting with a stutter"** — repeat a word or idea from the end of one unit at
  the start of the next ([AWAI](https://www.awai.com/2021/10/the-slippery-slide-technique-for-writing-web-copy/)).

That second one is literally the "words in a sentence" property you described, and it
is testable: **for each junction, can you name the word or idea that carries across?**
Where you can't, the thread is cut.

## B3 · Awareness stage — the constraint that outranks everything else

Eugene Schwartz's five stages: Unaware → Problem Aware → Solution Aware → Product Aware
→ Most Aware. The rule for ordering:

> *"A landing page built for cold traffic should not open with your product name…
> writing product-aware copy for a cold, unaware audience is the single biggest reason
> ads fail."* — [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/customer-awareness-stages)

> *"The further left a reader is, the less you may mention the product, and the more
> the ad has to earn attention through the reader's own situation."*

**This binds LEXFIT directly.** The homepage brief is explicitly *cold and wide*
(`05-homepage-wireframe.md`). So the early sections must work in the visitor's world —
their week, their flat, their time — before the page starts describing catalogue
scope, casting, and plan structure.

## B4 · The 2026 four-stage decision model

The most useful modern synthesis I found maps cleanly onto sections:

> Stage 1 **orientation** — "Is this relevant to my context?"
> Stage 2 **credibility** — "Can I trust this team and product?"
> Stage 3 **effort** — "How hard is adoption?"
> Stage 4 **commitment** — "Should I start now?"
>
> *"When pages skip stages or reorder them poorly, visitors delay commitment and
> continue evaluation elsewhere."*
> — [Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/saas-landing-page-conversion-system-in-2026/)

Note the order: **credibility comes second, before effort and before commitment.**

## B5 · Social proof placement

- Social proof lifts conversion by a **median 37%** (range 10–270%)
  ([Genesys Growth](https://genesysgrowth.com/blog/social-proof-conversion-stats-for-marketing-leaders)).
- **Place a credibility indicator directly below the hero** — user counts, a stat, or
  logos ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/social-proof-placement)).
- **Position the fuller proof mid-page — after the solution is introduced, before the
  final CTA** ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/social-proof-examples-for-landing-pages)).
- **3–5 testimonials** is the balance point between credibility and cognitive load
  ([ProveSrc](https://provesrc.com/blog/social-proof-landing-pages-best-practices/)).

## B6 · Length

> *"The best marketing SaaS landing pages use **5–8 sections** with a single primary
> CTA repeated throughout."* — [DesignRevision](https://designrevision.com/blog/marketing-saas-landing-pages)

**LEXFIT has 13.** Worth holding lightly — that benchmark is for SaaS lead-gen, and a
consumer subscription with a founder brand legitimately runs longer. But it is a real
signal that the page is carrying more sections than the pattern expects, and the
question "which sections should not exist" belongs in the reorder conversation
alongside "what order should they be in."

# PART C — Where the current thread holds and where it snaps

Applying B2's test to each junction: **name the idea that carries across.**

| Junction | Carrying idea | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 → 2 Hero → Hogyan | "összeállítom a tervem" → *this* is what that means | ✅ **strong** |
| 2 → 3 Hogyan → Finish | ⟂ none — jumps from *starting* to *finishing* | ❌ **snap** |
| 3 → 4 Finish → Alexával | ⟂ backwards — the payoff precedes the thing itself | ❌ **snap** |
| 4 → 5 Alexával → Nagy képernyő | "megnyomod a playt" → where you press it | ⚠️ weak |
| 5 → 6 Nagy képernyő → Programok | ⟂ none | ❌ **snap** |
| 6 → 7 Programok → Ár | "mind benne van" → ennyibe kerül | ✅ **strong** |
| 7 → 8 Ár → Foundation | "megvetted, hol kezdd" | ✅ good |
| 8 → 9 Foundation → A heted | "sorrendben" → "de te döntöd el, mikor" | ✅ **strong** |
| 9 → 10 A heted → Kihívások | "a te napjaid" → "amit együtt csinálunk" | ⚠️ weak |
| 10 → 11 Kihívások → Alexa | "a csoport" → aki a csoportot csinálja | ✅ **strong** |
| 11 → 12 Alexa → GYIK | ⟂ emotional peak → admin | ❌ **snap** |
| 12 → 13 GYIK → Előfizetés | objections cleared → choose a plan | ✅ **strong** |

**Six strong joins, two weak, four snaps.**

Three structural facts that the reorder discussion should start from:

1. **§3 Finish is currently marooned.** Moving it to position 3 today gained band-rhythm
   (it broke up a double-navy run) but cost thread: it now sits between the onboarding
   mechanism and the session itself, and creates snaps on *both* sides. It is the
   clearest single lever in the whole page.
2. **Authority arrives 11th of 13.** Schwartz (B3) and the four-stage model (B4) both put
   credibility early — stage 2 of 4. The founder story is the page's highest-trust
   asset and a cold visitor meets it after ~9,000px of scroll.
3. **The two social-proof sections never meet.** B5 says put a credibility marker under
   the hero and the fuller proof mid-page. LEXFIT has excellent material for both
   (seven real member photos; 1 200+ group members) and currently uses neither position.
