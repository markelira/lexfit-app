Don't fake a daily habit. Build a weekly one and defend it.

LEXFIT's programme prescribes 3–5 workouts a week. Nobody trains twice a day. So the honest version of the retention question is not "how do we make this a daily app" — it is "what happens on day 4, when there is nothing scheduled, and the user has to decide whether this is still part of their life." That decision is made roughly nine times in the first fourteen days. This document is about those nine moments.

TL;DR

The 14-day window is not a marketing period, it is the entire product. Users who complete fewer than three workouts in their first fourteen days churn at 3–4× the rate of those who establish a weekly rhythm. Sessions completed in week one are the single strongest predictor of long-term subscription. Everything else — renewal, LTV, referral — is downstream of that one number.

Copy the weekly commitment, not the daily streak. Down Dog switched from daily participation to flexible weekly goals and gained 20% in 90-day retention. LEXFIT's structural disadvantage (no daily workout) becomes an advantage the moment the unit of commitment matches the programme instead of fighting it.

Alexa is the moat, and she is currently underused. Pure tracking apps without human touchpoints see D90 retention below 10%; products with real human accountability reach 20–35%. LEXFIT has one named, real trainer and a 17,000-person Facebook group. That is a structural advantage Peloton cannot buy and Keep could not build.

Be careful with rewards. The Deci–Koestner–Ryan meta-analysis found that all expected tangible rewards contingent on performance reliably undermine intrinsic motivation — and a 2025 study of Keep found excessive gamification produces stress and emotional exhaustion. Points, badges and coins are the wrong instrument for a 35+ audience returning to movement.

Hungary-specific headwind: fewer than 30% of Hungarian consumers want subscription models at all — the lowest in CEE alongside Slovakia, versus 41% in Poland. The retention system must earn renewal behaviourally, because the cultural default is to resist recurring payment.

R1

The structural problem, stated honestly

Most retention advice assumes a daily-use product. LEXFIT is not one, and pretending otherwise produces the worst possible outcome: a user who feels they are failing an app that told them to rest.

A hamis kérdés

„Hogyan legyen ez napi használatú app?"

This leads directly to filler: step counters nobody asked for, calorie logs that get abandoned in week two, badges for opening the app. Users spend an average of 2.5 minutes a day in fitness apps — there is no room for invented daily busywork, and manufactured daily obligation on a rest day actively contradicts the coaching.

A valódi kérdés

„Miért nyitja meg a 4. napon, amikor nincs edzés?"

Because something is there — a plan that acknowledges the rest day, a word from Alexa, a visible weekly shape she is 3/5 through, a group that trained today. The rest day is not a gap to be filled with a game. It is a touchpoint to be honoured.

Why 14 days and not 30

Fitness apps have a novelty window of roughly 3–7 days where users open the app because it is new, not because a habit exists. Once the novelty ends, if the app has not given a concrete reason to return by day 10, it does not get one. The subscription's first billing cycle is therefore decided long before the renewal date — renewal is a lagging indicator of week one.

The counter-intuitive frequency finding

3–4 workouts per week is optimal for retention. More is better for results but worse for retention — it produces burnout. LEXFIT's onboarding asks how many days fit, and offers 3/4/5/6. The research says the app should gently steer a returning-to-movement beginner toward 3, not 5, and raise it later. An ambitious answer on day one is a churn risk on day nine.

R2

The Eastern model — and why Keep is a warning, not a template

China's approach is the most aggressive in the world: fitness as social media, commerce and content in one surface. It is instructive precisely because the market leader executed it fully and still lost retention.

What Keep built

Keep (2015, 36M MAU at peak) is barely recognisable as a workout app — the exercise-recording buttons are small and easy to overlook at the top of a landing page dominated by classes, feeds and products. It is closer to a fitness-focused Xiaohongshu: social networking, KOL-led series, equipment e-commerce, nutrition, its own gyms, and a WeChat mini-programme for frictionless entry. Its largest revenue line is e-commerce, not subscription.

What happened

The outcome

Keep's monthly retention peaked in 2020 and has since fallen below pre-pandemic levels. Only about 1 in 100 users buys anything in a given month. And the number that matters most for LEXFIT: the average Keep user exercises with the app once a week. Chinese media's diagnosis was blunt — with thousands of fitness KOLs giving away workouts on Douyin, "Keep is replaceable."

The lesson is not that social features don't work. It is that social features layered onto replaceable content do not create retention. Keep's competition was never other fitness apps — it was every free creator on Douyin, one of whom (Liu Genghong) drew larger audiences than Keep's entire MAU. Home-fitness video on Douyin grew 134% year-on-year in 2021; Kuaishou had 600,000+ fitness livestreamers.

What is genuinely worth stealing from the East

Livestream co-presence

The one mechanic that outperformed gamification: live comments and reactions create a sense of community that recorded content cannot. Not that LEXFIT needs livestream — but "other people are doing this right now" is a real, cheap retention primitive.

The person, not the platform

China's home-fitness winners were individuals with audiences, not apps with libraries. Alexa is the asset. A product that foregrounds the library over the trainer is competing on replaceability.

Where it broke

A 2025 study of Keep's gamification found fun and achievement raise persistence short-term, but excessive gamification causes stress and emotional exhaustion. The Chinese model's ceiling is a design warning.

R3

The Western model — what actually creates pull

Duolingo is the most instructive case in consumer software, and the popular reading of it is wrong. The streak is not a notification strategy. It is an architecture decision.

The three findings that matter

W-01

The streak sits inside the core loop, not on top of it.

Duolingo treats the streak as one of the rewards that fires when the core action completes — action → reward → progress. It is not a retention layer bolted on afterwards, which is why it never competes with the reason users opened the app.

Why it matters for LEXFITA streak that only exists in the header is decoration. It has to be part of what completing a workout gives you.
W-02

Identity attachment outperforms behavioural habit.

Users who identify as streak-maintainers are more resilient to a break than users who are merely afraid of losing progress. Identity formation is described as the highest-durability retention state — users don't just use Duolingo, they are Duolingo users.

NumbersUsers reaching a 7-day streak are 3.6× more likely to complete their course and 2.4× more likely to return the next day.
W-03

A break is a recoverable state, not a failure.

Someone who built a habit and lost it circumstantially is a demonstrably high-value user who can rebuild it. Duolingo's comeback mechanics target exactly that segment, and streak freezes function as a safety net rather than a licence to skip.

DisciplineDuolingo has run 600+ experiments on the streak alone — roughly one every other day for four years — with an explicit rule that any monetisation change hurting Day-7 retention gets rolled back.
The insight the user asked about — pull without push

Duolingo's Friend Streaks give it a notification stream that is structurally harder to mute than its own algorithmic pushes: "It's not an owl asking you to come back — it's your friend asking where you've been." The app could send the identical message. The sender changes the meaning. This is the entire answer to "how do TikTok and Instagram retain without notifying": the pull comes from other people, not from the product.

R4

The science — and the trap most fitness apps walk into

Self-Determination Theory is the best-evidenced framework for exercise adherence, and it contains a direct warning about the gamification playbook.

Autonómia

Choice with minimal pressure

Supported by helping someone decide for personal reasons. Critically: competence does not produce intrinsic motivation in the absence of autonomy. A user who feels the app is instructing rather than offering gets no motivational benefit from getting better.

Kompetencia

Structure, feedback, realistic goals

The 8-week Foundation programme and the visszamérés are textbook competence support — provided the goals are achievable. This is the argument for steering a beginner to 3 days rather than 5.

Kapcsolódás

Connectedness to others

Feeling socially included and supported facilitates intrinsic motivation. This is the Facebook group's real job — and the reason removing all social presence from the app was a risk worth naming.

The trap

A comprehensive meta-analysis (Deci, Koestner & Ryan) confirmed that all expected tangible rewards made contingent on task performance reliably undermine intrinsic motivation. And it is not only rewards: threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations and imposed goals diminish it the same way, because they shift the perceived locus of causality outward. Extrinsic motivation can change behaviour short-term but fails to maintain it over time.

The finding that should shape LEXFIT's copy

Exercise motives are not equal. Body-related motives — weight, appearance — are experienced as extrinsic and internally controlling. Affiliation and skill development are experienced as autonomous. And the temporal split matters enormously: well-internalised extrinsic motives drive initial adoption (they are why someone subscribes), while a predominance of intrinsic motivation drives longer-term participation.

What this means concretely

The first fourteen days are the handover — from "I want to lose weight" (why she paid) to "I like how this feels and it is mine" (why she stays). A retention system that keeps hammering the weight goal is optimising for the motive that predicts churn. This is the strongest possible evidence for the non-scale-wins block and the „Miért kezdted" sentence already in the design — and for why „Megcsináltad" beats „−0,4 kg".

R5

Localising to Hungary and CEE

A fő akadály

Fewer than 30% of Hungarians want subscriptions

PwC's CEE Voice of the Consumer found Hungary and Slovakia at under 30% willingness to move to subscription models — against 41% in Poland and 47% in Ukraine, and a 35% regional average. Poland leads the region on subscription attitude; Hungary trails it. Cultural, economic and payment-infrastructure maturity all contribute.

A kedvező adat

Digital fitness converts unusually well

Trial-to-paid conversion for digital fitness is 63.7% — about 12 points above other digital subscription categories. And fitness churn improved from 8.9% (2023) to 7.2% (2025), with the B2C improvement attributed substantially to flexible pause features.

Four consequences for the Hungarian market

  • Pause must exist, and be easy to find. In a market that resists recurring payment, a visible „szüneteltetés" is a retention feature, not a leak. Cancel-or-continue is a binary Hungarian users will resolve toward cancel; pause-or-continue is not.
  • The annual plan carries disproportionate weight. With 39 900 Ft/year at −44%, the annual plan removes eleven monthly renewal decisions in a market predisposed to say no to each one. Sunk-cost commitment cuts both ways ethically — but the honest version is real value plus real savings, which is what is on the pricing page.
  • The Facebook group is a local advantage, not a legacy channel. CEE consumers engage heavily through social media, and a 17,000-member Hungarian-language group around a named trainer is not something a global competitor can replicate. Apple Fitness+ has no Hungarian Alexa.
  • Price sensitivity is elevated. CEE consumers in 2024 were carefully weighing finances in retail choices. Per-week framing (767 Ft/hét) is doing real work — keep it.
GDPR note, briefly

Several mechanics in the American playbook — visible friend activity, leaderboards, social streaks — involve processing that needs a clear lawful basis and genuine opt-in under GDPR. Progress photos are special-category-adjacent. The design decision already taken (photos private by construction, no follower counts) is defensible both as UX and as compliance posture. Do not add social visibility without a privacy review.

R6

The recommendation — a 14-day system for LEXFIT

Six mechanics, ordered by evidence strength per unit of build effort. None of them is a points system. All of them are things a real coach would do.

01

The unit of commitment is the week, not the day.

The ring reads 3/5 ezen a héten, never a daily obligation. A rest day is drawn as part of the plan and explicitly does not break anything. The user chooses the weekly number and can change it — autonomy support, not an imposed goal.

EvidenceDown Dog: +20% D90 from flexible weekly goals. SDT: imposed goals diminish intrinsic motivation the same way rewards do.
02

Three workouts in the first seven days is the only activation metric that matters.

Instrument it, alert on it, and design the first week backwards from it. If a user is at 1 by day 5, that is the moment for intervention — not day 25. The first week's plan should be the three shortest, easiest sessions in the programme, regardless of the level they selected.

Evidence<3 workouts in 14 days → 3–4× churn. First-week sessions are the strongest single predictor of long-term subscription.
03

Alexa appears personally in the first fourteen days — at least twice, as a human.

Not an automated „Hajrá!". A short video or voice note addressed to the cohort that started this week, and a genuine response to the „Miért kezdted" sentence. This is the highest-leverage unbuilt feature in the product.

EvidencePure-app D90 <10% vs 20–35% with human accountability. The hybrid model (automated daily, human weekly) is what Noom and others converged on.
04

The rest day gets content, not a game.

A 60-second action at most: one line from Alexa, a non-scale-win prompt, tomorrow's session previewed, or a 3-minute mobility option that is explicitly optional. Under a minute, and never framed as an obligation. The rest day's job is to keep the app in her life without contradicting the coaching.

EvidenceTiny Habits: design the minimum daily action for <60s completion. Apps that educate about recovery see 20% higher adherence because users feel validated rather than guilty.
05

Streak with forgiveness, framed as consistency — and a one-tap comeback.

Rest days protect it. One free „freeze" per month. When it does break, the copy is „Új sorozat indul" with one-tap restart, never a red zero. Someone who broke a 12-day streak is a high-value user, not a failure.

Evidence7-day streak → 3.6× course completion, 2.4× next-day return. Streak freezes remove punishment while keeping the habit; breaks are a recoverable state.
06

Co-presence, borrowed rather than built.

„312-en csinálták végig ezt a hetet" and „ma 47 valaki edzett veled együtt" — facts about other people, no leaderboard, no follower counts. Plus the challenge archive as a Strava-style time-bounded event. The Facebook group stays the live room; the app carries the evidence that she is not alone.

EvidenceFriend-sender effect: social weight the app cannot manufacture. Strava Challenges: D90 18%→32%. SDT relatedness. Social features raise retention ~30%.

One design change this research suggests

The Haladásom design places the first visszamérés at week 3 and the big one at week 8. Consider a small, honest measurement at day 14. The evidence says the retention decision is made inside the first two weeks — so the proof has to arrive inside the first two weeks too. Not the full re-test; something small and real that says „ez működik".

R7

What we should deliberately not do

✕Points, coins or badges for opening the app. Expected tangible rewards contingent on behaviour reliably undermine intrinsic motivation. This is the single best-evidenced "don't" in the document.
✕Leaderboards ranking users against each other. The slowest members leave first, and they are the audience. Facts about the group, yes; rankings, no.
✕A daily obligation on rest days. It contradicts the coaching, and apps that validate rest see 20% higher adherence.
✕Manufactured logging (calories, weight, steps). Abandoned in week two, and body-related metrics are the motive class that predicts churn.
✕Guilt-based notifications. Duolingo earned the right to be persistent because users bought into the streak first. Escalating urgency before buy-in reads as harassment.
✕Steering beginners to 5–6 days a week. More frequency is better for results and worse for retention. Let her succeed at 3 first.
✕Hiding cancellation. In a market already sceptical of subscriptions, a dark-pattern cancel flow poisons word-of-mouth in a 17,000-person group. Offer pause prominently instead.
✕Building a social feed in the app. Keep did all of it and still lost retention. The group already exists; the app should carry evidence of it, not compete with it.

A process recommendation, not a feature

Duolingo ran 600+ experiments on the streak alone, with a named person empowered to veto anything that cheapened it, and a standing rule that any change hurting Day-7 retention is rolled back. LEXFIT does not need that volume — but it needs the rule: one metric (3 workouts in 7 days), one owner, and permission to reject features that trade it for short-term engagement.
