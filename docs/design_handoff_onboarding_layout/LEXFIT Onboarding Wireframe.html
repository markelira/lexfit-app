LEXFIT · Dashboard skeleton · Onboarding + Auth

Ask only what the product will actually use.

Onboarding is the last thing to design, not the first — because until the screens exist you cannot know what they need. Now they exist, and every question can be traced to a place it appears. The existing flow asks nine question screens before the paywall; the app currently consumes five answers. This document proposes the sequence, the dependency map behind it, and where the existing LEXFIT Auth page sits in it.

Model · Duolingo / Nike Training Club onboarding Auth · the existing split-screen page, unchanged Order · value → questions → plan → account → payment Mobile-first · one question per screen

Nine screens → five

Lean onboarding is the pattern in the reference set — Nike Training Club's flow is six steps. Every removed question is a place the user does not abandon.

Progressive profiling

Cut questions are deferred, not deleted. Focus after week 1, obstacle after the first missed week, life stage where variations are offered.

One addition

"Miért kezdted" is quoted on two screens already drawn and captured nowhere. It is the only new question this document adds.

Life stage needs care

Pregnancy and postpartum change what is safe. Deferring it is a judgement call, not a safety call — if the programme adapts materially, it must be asked up front instead.

O2

The sequence — and where Auth sits

Account creation comes after the plan is revealed. The user answers five quick questions, sees the week that was built for them, and only then is asked for an email — at the moment the thing being signed up for is concrete. This is the existing Auth page's own assumption: its register pane already says „a csomagot a következő lépésben választod ki".

Answering five taps costs nothing and builds investment; typing an email costs something and is easier to refuse. By step 9 the user has a plan with their name on it — the account is now the way to keep it, not a toll gate.

Amit az Auth oldal már feltételez
Nincs ár az auth oldalon.

Its handoff is explicit: pricing was deliberately removed and the register lede promises plan selection "in the next step". That only makes sense with checkout after auth — which is exactly this order.

Defer sign-up

The Duolingo pattern: let people taste the value before asking who they are. Here the taste is the generated week.

Auth page unchanged

No redesign needed. It is already high-fidelity and structurally correct — this document only fixes where in the flow it appears.

Answers survive the account

The five answers are held locally and attached on registration. A user who bounces at auth and returns must not be re-interrogated.

Free-tier question

If any workout is ever free, the natural place is between step 8 and 9 — do one before signing up. Not drawn; depends on a business decision.

O3

The question screen — one pattern, five times

One question per screen, a progress bar that shows the end is near, a back arrow that always works, and options big enough to hit without looking. Nothing on these screens is novel: this is the shape of every onboarding the audience has completed in the last five years.

One question per screen

The Duolingo / NTC shape. It keeps each decision small, makes the back button meaningful, and lets the progress bar tell the truth.

Progress bar with a visible end

"3 / 5" beside a segmented bar. An unbounded flow is the single biggest predictor of abandonment in a question sequence.

Options are 56px tall

52px for a single line, ~68px when an explanation is shown — both well above the 44px floor. Radio semantics on single-choice, checkbox on multi — the tick shape differs accordingly.

Show the consequence immediately

Choosing 5 days draws the week strip on that screen. The user sees the product being built rather than filling in a form.

Icons, not emoji

The existing flow uses emoji (💪🔥🌱). Drawn here with the Lucide set for consistency with the app and legibility for a 35+ audience — but this is a brand-voice call, not a research verdict.

The reveal is the receipt

It restates the answers as a plan — "heti 5 edzés · reggelente · csendes variációkkal". The user should recognise their own inputs, otherwise the questions felt pointless.

"Mentsük el a tervedet"

The button into auth names what registering protects. Not "Regisztráció" — the account is a means, the plan is the thing.

Permission asked in context

The notification prompt appears after the first home screen, with the time already filled from the napszak answer, and "Most nem" as a real option.

Price stated once, plainly

Three plans, one selected, and the CTA repeats the exact amount being charged — „Előfizetek — 39 900 Ft / év". The existing sales-page audit flagged price clarity as a P1; do not repeat that here.

The live pricing model

Heti 490 Ft (első 7 nap, utána 1990 Ft/hét) · Havi 5990 Ft · Éves 39 900 Ft (767 Ft/hét, −44%). Taken from the pricing page. Note onboarding/onb-data.jsx still hard-codes 19 990 Ft / hó — that is stale and needs correcting.

Select, then one button

Three cards each with their own CTA is the desktop pricing-table convention; on mobile the familiar pattern is radio-style selection plus a single fixed action. Keeps RULE 05 — one loud button per screen.

Normalised to a week

Every plan also shown per week, so the comparison is like-for-like. That is what makes „SPÓROLJ 44%" verifiable rather than a claim.

One shell, two contents

The brand panel never moves between step 1 and step 9. The auth page stops being a separate destination and becomes the last card in a sequence the user is already inside.

One correction the auth page needs

Its form is used exactly as built. But the brand panel still reads „4 hét / 100+ edzés / a közösségben", while Foundation is eight weeks and the library is 200+ everywhere else in this skeleton — and „közösség" is now the Facebook group. Drawn here with the corrected figures; LEXFIT Auth.html needs the same edit.

The mobile decision the handoff flagged

Its README warns that stacking pushes the form below the fold under 940px. In this flow that is solved for free — mobile steps never show the brand panel, only the mark. Worth confirming that is the chosen answer.

O6

Onboarding rules

O-RULE 01

No question without a consumer.

Every question maps to a screen that displays or uses its answer. If it maps to nothing, it waits.

EvidenceThe dependency map in O1 — nine asked, five used.
O-RULE 02

Value before account, account before payment.

Five questions → the plan → registration → checkout.

EvidenceDeferred sign-up is the Duolingo pattern; the Auth page's own copy already assumes checkout comes after it.
O-RULE 03

One question per screen, with a visible end.

Segmented progress bar plus "3 / 5". Back always available and non-destructive.

EvidenceThe shape of every onboarding this audience has completed recently.
O-RULE 04

Show the consequence on the same screen.

Choosing 5 days draws the week; choosing "csendben" says what changes.

EvidenceMakes the form feel like building rather than filling; reduces the "why are you asking" drop-off.
O-RULE 05

The reveal restates the answers.

The plan screen quotes the inputs back so the questions visibly paid off.

EvidencePeak–end — the reveal is the emotional peak of the flow and its justification.
O-RULE 06

Permissions are asked in context, later.

Notifications after the first home screen, pre-filled from the napszak answer, with a real "Most nem".

EvidencePermission language must state why; up-front prompts are refused and never re-asked.

What we are deliberately not doing

✕Asking for an email first. The most refusable request placed where the user has invested nothing.
✕Nine question screens. Four of them currently feed nothing. Defer, don't delete.
✕An unbounded flow. No progress bar means no idea when it ends — the strongest abandonment predictor here.
✕A notification permission prompt before the first workout. Refused by default, and refusals are permanent.
✕Pricing on the auth page. Removed deliberately in that redesign; do not reintroduce it.
✕Re-asking the questions after a bounce at auth. Answers are held locally and attached on registration.
Still to settle

Three: whether életszakasz (pregnancy / postpartum / menopause) is safety-critical enough to belong up front rather than deferred — a trainer question, not a design one; whether the option icons use Lucide or the existing emoji; and whether a free first workout sits between the reveal and registration, which depends on a business decision rather than this skeleton.
