LEXFIT · Dashboard skeleton · Haladásom

Proof, not encouragement.

Every other screen in the product asks the user to do something. This one owes them something back. It answers three questions in descending order of frequency — am I on track this week, am I building a habit, am I actually stronger than I was — and the third one is the reason the whole 8-week programme exists. Structure only.

H1

Three questions, in frequency order

The page is stacked by how often each question is asked, not by how impressive the answer looks. A user opens Haladásom most days to check the week; occasionally to admire the streak; and four times in eight weeks to find out whether the programme worked. The layout has to serve all three without any of them needing a tab.

Kérdés 01 · szinte minden nap
„Jól állok a héten?"

Answered by the ring and the week strip, above the fold, in under a second. This is the default reason the screen is opened.

Kérdés 02 · hetente
„Tartom a szokást?"

Answered by the streak, the total minutes and the 8-week bar chart. Habit is a shape over time, not a number.

Kérdés 03 · 4× nyolc hét alatt
„Tényleg erősebb lettem?"

Answered only by the visszamérés: the same workout as week 1, measured. Rare, but it is the reason the programme exists.

Visszamérés
RULE 07

Ring, big tabular numbers, week strip. The "am I on track" model is already installed by Apple Fitness and banking apps — reuse it rather than invent a visualisation.

No tabs on this page

All three answers live on one scroll. A tabbed progress screen hides the rare-but-important third question behind a control nobody clicks.

The trap

Progress screens attract charts. Every chart added here competes with the three questions above — if a graphic does not answer one of them, it does not ship.

Ring + strip, side by side

The two answers to question one, together, above the fold. Everything below is optional reading.

Every number has a comparison

"+1 a múlt héthez", "a leghosszabb eddig". A bare number is a fact; a number with a direction is progress. Banking dashboards do this on every figure.

Future weeks are visible but faded

The 8-week chart shows where the user is in the arc, not just what they have done. Milestones use the same locked treatment.

Rest days are stated, not implied

"A pihenőnap nem töri meg a sorozatot" sits under the strip, where the anxiety actually occurs.

Absent on purpose

No calories, no weight, no percentile against other users. Each would answer a question nobody on this programme asked.

H3

The visszamérés — the payoff moment

Week 8 repeats the week 1 workout exactly. This is the single most important screen in the product, and its design job is unusual: not to encourage, but to prove. The user does not have to believe they improved — they read the difference. Three states: locked, ready, result.

Következő 8 hétMegosztás
Same workout, same clock

The comparison is only credible because the variable is controlled. The design has to say that out loud — "ugyanannyi idő alatt" is doing as much work as the number.

Locked ≠ hidden

State A is visible from week 1 with a "?" in the second box. The unanswered question is the motivation; hiding it wastes seven weeks of anticipation.

Peak–end

People remember an experience by its peak and its ending. This screen is the peak of the eight weeks — it earns more space and more of Alexa's voice than anything else in the product.

Sharing is offered, never automatic

Secondary button, after the result. Solo experience first, social as an optional layer.

What if the number is worse?

Not drawn yet, and it must be. Illness, holidays and bad weeks are normal. A design that only works when the number improves is not finished — see the open questions.

H4

Non-scale wins and private photos

The programme's own framing is that the scale is not the measure. Two blocks carry that: wins in the user's own words, and progress photos that are private by construction — stated as a fact, not offered as a setting.

The user's words, dated

Non-scale wins are quoted, not paraphrased into product copy. They tie back to the "Miért kezdted" sentence captured at onboarding.

Privacy is stated, not toggled

A setting implies it could be otherwise. Photos are owner-only by construction; the sentence says so before the user uploads anything.

Milestone slots, not a gallery

Week 1 / 5 / 8, with future slots locked. Three deliberate moments beat an unbounded camera roll.

Sensitive by default

Body photos are the highest-anxiety feature in the product. Nothing here should ever be one tap from a share sheet.

Comparison stacks vertically

On a phone the before/after becomes top/bottom with the delta between them. Side-by-side at 320px would shrink both numbers below the point of impact.

The visszamérés card is on screen one

Even locked. It is the only element that makes the other numbers mean something.

Tab bar stays

Including on the result screen. This is a moment, not a modal takeover — the user should be able to walk away from it freely.

H6

Haladásom rules

H-RULE 01

Ring and week strip above the fold, always.

Question one is asked most days and must be answered in under a second.

EvidenceRULE 07 — the Apple Fitness / banking model for "am I on track" is already installed.
H-RULE 02

Every number carries a direction.

"+1 a múlt héthez", "a leghosszabb eddig". A bare figure is a fact; a compared figure is progress.

EvidenceStandard across banking and fitness dashboards; the comparison is what users actually read.
H-RULE 03

The visszamérés is visible from week 1.

Locked, with a "?" where the answer will go. The open question is the motivation.

EvidencePeak–end rule: the peak of the eight weeks deserves seven weeks of anticipation.
H-RULE 04

Prove, don't cheer.

Same workout, same clock, stated explicitly. Alexa's line interprets the number; it never replaces it.

EvidenceThe programme's own framing — "nem hinned kell, hogy fejlődtél, mérni fogod".
H-RULE 05

Photos are private by construction.

Stated before upload, never one tap from sharing, milestone slots only.

EvidencePrivacy anxiety is the top-cited concern in fitness-app UX reviews.
H-RULE 06

No chart that does not answer one of the three questions.

If a graphic cannot be traced to H1, it does not ship.

EvidenceCognitive load — avoid visual clutter, build on existing models.

What we are deliberately not doing

✕Weight or BMI as a headline. The programme measures against week 1, not against a scale — a weight chart would contradict the product's own framing.
✕Calorie estimates. Unverifiable without a wearable, and they invite exactly the accounting mindset the programme avoids.
✕Percentile rankings against other users. A progress page is about one person; comparison has no home in this product.
✕Breaking the streak on a planned rest day. The forgiveness mechanic is the reason streaks survive real life.
✕A tabbed progress screen. It would hide the visszamérés — the rarest and most important answer — behind a control nobody taps.
✕Auto-sharing results. Offered as a secondary button, after the fact, never as a default.
Still to settle — one of these matters more than the rest

The visszamérés when the number is flat or worse. Illness, holidays and hard weeks are normal, and a screen that only works on improvement will fail the users who most need it. Options to explore: lead with consistency ("0 kihagyott hét") instead of the delta, surface the non-scale wins in place of the comparison, or let Alexa reframe explicitly. This needs a design pass and a real conversation with Alexa about tone — it should not be improvised in build.
