LEXFIT · Dashboard skeleton · Mobile expansion

The phone is the product.

Desktop was drawn first because it is easier to reason about; mobile is where the sessions actually happen — often propped against a wall with a mat already down. This document expands the mobile side of the skeleton: thirteen screens, the thumb-reach model behind them, and the rules that keep them familiar. Structure only, Variant B cards, Lucide icons.

Nav · fixed bottom tab bar, 4 destinations Targets · 44×44px minimum, everywhere Card · Variant B, one per row-width with peek Icons · Lucide (ISC)
M1

The mobile chassis and the thumb

Three fixed layers: a slim top bar for identity and search, a scrolling content area, and a bottom tab bar that never moves. The reason the tabs are at the bottom is not fashion — it is Fitts's law: the time to hit a target is a function of its distance and size, and the bottom third of the screen is the only region the thumb reaches without regripping.

RULE 06 · F-07

Fixed bottom tabs, four destinations, icon + permanent label. Apple HIG has long recommended the tab bar; Material moved to bottom nav for primary destinations in 2016.

Fitts's law

The bottom band is the cheapest real estate on the device. Primary actions live there; the top bar holds only identity and search.

Hidden-nav penalty

NN/g measured mobile users on hidden navigation at 57% usage vs 86% for partly-visible, and 15% slower on task. Visible tabs are the whole point.

Search stays reachable

The field is in the top bar of every screen — it does not retreat into Videótár on small screens. Top-right placement is the engraved convention even when the strip is narrow.

Target floor

44×44px minimum on every tab, save control and play button. Tabs are 64px tall for this reason, not for visual weight.

M2

Kezdőlap — above the fold, then the rows

The first screenful must answer one question: what am I doing today, and can I start it now? Hero, one loud button, then the week. Rows begin below the fold — that is correct, because a user with a plan never needs to scroll, and a user browsing always will.

Kihívások
One loud action

"Edzés indítása" is the only filled button on the screen, full width, 44px tall, sitting in the thumb zone. Nielsen: a quiet primary CTA gets overlooked because every other app shouts.

Rows: one card + peek

Cards are 72% of the row width so the next one always peeks. Users judge a row by its first thumbnails and swipe only when committed — the peek is what tells them swiping is possible.

Week strip above the rows

Ring-and-strip is the Apple Fitness / banking model for "am I on track". It stays above the fold; the rows do not.

Rest day protects the streak

The humane version of Duolingo's freeze: the flame does not break, and Alexa says so explicitly. Freeze-equipped streaks reportedly run ~48% longer past day seven.

Deliberate omission

No notification bell, no badge counts, no "new" dots on the home screen. Every additional signal competes with the one button that matters.

M3

Keresés and Videótár — browse, then results

Two modes, the YouTube split: rows while browsing, a vertical result list the moment intent is expressed. Results are one column, full width — legibility beats density once the user has already told us what they want.

Kihívások
Browse vs. results

Rows while exploring; a one-column list the moment a query or filter exists. The same switch YouTube makes, and users already expect it.

Bottom sheet for filters

Filters open from the bottom, in the thumb zone, with a confirming button that reports the result count before committing. Sheets are the standard iOS and Material mobile disclosure.

Chips are 44px tall

Filter chips look small but their tap area is not. This is the most commonly failed target on the existing Apple TV page audit.

Recent searches, not suggestions

Recency beats cleverness on a small keyboard. NN/g: people are notoriously bad at formulating queries, and typing on mobile is expensive.

M4

The workout flow — detail sheet, player, done

The path from tapping a card to finishing a workout is the only flow the product truly needs to get right. Three moments, each borrowed: the detail sheet from Spotify/Apple Music, the player from the YouTube watch page — video pinned to the top, content scrolling beneath, tab bar intact — and the completion screen from Duolingo's lesson end.

Megosztás
Detail sheet, not a page

Tapping a card opens a bottom sheet over the current screen, so dismissing returns you exactly where you were. Spotify and Apple Music both work this way; a full page navigation loses the user's place.

Player = the YouTube watch page

Video pinned at the top, everything else scrolling beneath it, tab bar still visible. Controls appear on tap and fade — exactly the sequence the audience performs dozens of times a week.

Chapters → blocks

YouTube's segmented chapter scrubber maps onto bemelegítés · fő blokk · levezetés without inventing anything. Skip ±10s becomes the natural exercise-repeat control.

Where the slots are re-aimed

Title and meta stay put. YouTube's like/share pill row becomes Mentés · TV-re — only the two actions a workout actually needs; the channel row becomes Alexa ("4 hete együtt"); the description becomes the live countdown strip; up-next becomes "Mai menü".

The countdown is below the video, not over it

Overlaying a timer would obscure the demonstration and break the tap-to-reveal convention. It sits where YouTube's title block sits — always visible, never in the way.

Open question

Whether "Mai menü" should auto-scroll to keep the current exercise centred, or stay put so the user can look ahead. Both behaviours exist in the market.

The completion screen is the reward

Check, what you did, the streak, the week, one line from Alexa. A finished workout is an achievement — never dimmed, never silent.

Sharing is optional and second

A great solo experience first; social as a secondary layer for those who want it.

Open question

Whether the player should keep the video visible during rest intervals or switch to a full-screen countdown. Both patterns exist in the market — worth testing.

Profile is a sheet, not a tab

Four tabs is the sweet spot; a fifth for profile would crowd the bar. The avatar stays top-right — the most engraved position — and opens a sheet in the thumb zone.

Haladásom · ring first

The Szavazz Magadra weeks, kept and re-playable. The voting stays in the Facebook group; the app is the shelf. See the Kihívások skeleton.

M6

Mobile rules

M-RULE 01

Four tabs, fixed, labelled, forever.

Icon above a permanently visible word. The active tab is filled. No fifth tab, no hiding on scroll.

EvidenceApple HIG tab-bar preference; Material's 2016 shift; NN/g hidden-nav penalty of 15% slower tasks on mobile.
M-RULE 02

Everything you tap lives in the bottom third.

Primary buttons full-width above the tab bar; sheets rise from the bottom; destructive or rare actions may sit high.

EvidenceFitts's law — time to acquire a target is a function of distance and size.
M-RULE 03

44×44px, no exceptions.

Chips, save buttons, tabs, transport controls. Visual size may be smaller than tap size.

EvidenceWCAG 2.5.8 / Apple HIG 44pt; already flagged as a failure on the existing Apple TV page.
M-RULE 04

One card wide, with the next one peeking.

72% of the row width. The peek is the only thing that tells a user the row scrolls.

EvidenceSIGIR '26 — users judge a row by its first thumbnails and swipe only when committed.
M-RULE 05

Sheets, not new pages, for anything you dismiss.

Detail, filters, profile. The user's place in the scroll is never lost.

EvidenceStandard iOS/Material disclosure; Spotify and Apple Music both use it for track detail.
M-RULE 06

Search never retreats.

Present in the top bar of every screen, even when the strip is tight.

EvidenceNielsen lists the open search box, top and right, as a canonical Jakob's Law pattern.

What we are deliberately not doing on mobile

✕A hamburger menu anywhere. Mobile hidden nav measured 57% usage vs 86% for partly-visible, and 15% slower on task.
✕Hiding the tab bar on scroll. Saves 64px, costs the user's map of the product.
✕A floating action button. There is already one loud action per screen; an FAB would compete with it and obscure content.
✕Gesture-only navigation with no visible control. Swipe may be an accelerator, never the only route.
✕Icon-only tabs once users "learn" the app. Labels are permanent — the audience skews 35+, and 65+ users are 43% slower on the web.
✕Two-column card grids on the home screen. Halves the artwork at exactly the moment the artwork is doing the work.
