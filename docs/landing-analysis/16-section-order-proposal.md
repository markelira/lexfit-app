## 0 · What that input changes — including a correction

`15-section-order-analysis.md` §C listed as a structural problem:

> *"Authority arrives 11th of 13. Both Schwartz and the four-stage model put
> credibility early. The founder story is the page's highest-trust asset and a cold
> visitor meets it after ~9,000px."*

**That was reasoning from the wrong premise, and it's wrong.** For an audience that
already knows Alexa, **stage 2 is largely pre-paid before they arrive.** They are not
asking *"can I trust this person"* — they answered that on Instagram or in the group.
Putting the founder story early would spend a full band re-proving something already
believed.

But stage 2 doesn't disappear — **it changes subject**:

| | Cold visitor asks | Warm visitor asks |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 2 | Can I trust *this person*? | Is *the product* real and finished? |

That is a completely different evidence set. Personal credibility is Alexa's story.
**Product credibility is real app screenshots, a real catalogue, real member cards** —
all of which LEXFIT has, and none of which is currently doing stage-2 work.

Schwartz agrees from the other direction: the further **right** on the awareness
scale, the more you may lead with the product and the offer. A warm audience is
solution-aware; leading with scope and mechanism is correct for them and would have
been wrong for a cold one.

# TASK 1 — The spine

Four stages, mapped to LEXFIT for a warm audience, with a section budget.

### Stage 1 · ORIENTATION — *"what has she actually built?"* — 1 section
They know the person, not the product. This needs to be **short**. A cold page would
spend three sections earning the right to describe the product; here it's one.

### Stage 2 · CREDIBILITY — *"is this real, or a PDF with a logo?"* — 3 sections
Reframed to **product** credibility. The evidence is: it's a working app (real
screens), it has real depth (7 programs / 47 workouts), and it costs a stated amount
(price as a credibility signal — a real product has a real price).

### Stage 3 · EFFORT — *"will I actually use it?"* — 3 sections
**The warm audience's real blocker.** They don't doubt Alexa; they doubt themselves.
Every objection here is about sustainability: my flat is small, I don't know where to
start, I don't have five evenings a week. This is where the page should be heaviest.

### Stage 4 · COMMITMENT — *"should I start now?"* — 5 sections
Payoff, community, remaining objections, the emotional push, the plans.

**Budget: 12 sections.** More on that in Task 2.

# TASK 2 — Triage and cuts

### CUT — `§5 Nagy képernyő` (1,062px)

The only position-independent cut I can justify. Nobody subscribes to a fitness app
because it casts to a TV. It is friction-removal for someone **already converted**,
which means it is doing stage-4 work with stage-2 real estate. Scored **L** on
conversion in the inventory — the only section that did.

**Don't lose the fact, lose the band.** "A böngészőben fut, nem kell letöltened
semmit" is genuinely useful and belongs as a line in §2 or a chip elsewhere. The
Chromecast/AirPlay detail can live in the GYIK, where it already half-lives
("Megy TV-n vagy laptopon is?").

### KEEP, but re-pointed — `Kihívások`

Worth flagging: for a **warm** audience, this section tells people about a Facebook
group **they are already in.** Its community-proof value drops sharply. What survives
is the part that is genuinely new to them — *the 15-challenge archive, organised and
searchable inside the app.* Its job changes from "we have a community" to "**the thing
you already do, now with a real archive**". That's a Task-4 rewrite, noted here.

### Two merge candidates I could NOT justify

I tried to reach the 9–10 sections I suggested and couldn't without losing a real
argument. Being straight about that rather than cutting to hit a number:

- **`Foundation` into `Programok`** — Foundation already appears as one of the seven
  banners. But its band answers "where do I start", which is a different question from
  "what's in it", and it's a top-3 objection for this audience. Kept. It is the best
  candidate if you want to push to 11.
- **`Finish Share` into `Kihívások`** — both are the social/emotional layer. But one is
  a private personal artefact and the other is a group activity; merging muddles both.
  Also, you asked for Finish Share to get *more* attention today, not less.

**Result: 13 → 12 sections.** The 5–8 benchmark is SaaS lead-gen; a consumer
subscription with a founder brand and 47 workouts legitimately runs longer. I'd rather
defend 12 than pretend to 8.

### Why Alexa moved to 11 rather than staying at 10

I tested both. Keeping `…Kihívások → Alexa → GYIK → Előfizetés` preserves the strong
group→founder link but leaves the snap that already exists — an emotional peak
dropping into admin. Putting GYIK first costs one weak seam and buys the page its best
possible ending: **the founder's "Kezdjük együtt" landing directly on the plans.**
Emotional → transactional is the classic close, and it removes the last snap.

### Why Finish Share moved to 8

It was band 9, I moved it to band 3 this morning at your request, and that created
snaps on **both** sides — the payoff arrived before the thing it pays off. At 8 it
finally has a real thread: *"you pick your days" → "and when you're done…" →
"…you share it in the group."* It sits between the two seams it strengthens most.

## What this order costs — stated, not hidden

**Band colours no longer alternate.** The current cream/navy rhythm was tuned to the
old sequence. Under the proposed order:

```
1 sage · 2 cream · 3 cream · 4 NAVY · 5 cream · 6 cream
· 7 NAVY · 8 NAVY · 9 cream · 10 cream · 11 PHOTO · 12 sage
```

Two cream-cream runs and one navy-navy run. **This is cheap to fix** — the ground is a
class (`.band-cream` / `.band-navy`), not content — but it is real work and it must be
redone as part of the reorder, not after. Per `12-apple-type-consistency-research.md`,
ground-flipping is where this page does most of its visual-variety work; leaving it
broken would undo a lot.

**§4 Ár-horgony now sits inside stage 2, not stage 4.** Deliberate: for a warm,
solution-aware audience a stated price is a credibility signal and a fast filter. But
it means the page names its price at ~25% scroll depth. If that feels early to you,
the alternative is moving it to 10 and losing the catalogue anchor — that anchor was
built on purpose and I'd argue to keep it.
